I have been in the software development/engineering field for about a decade now. 
My journey in the field has been guided by the writings and videos from other people in the field. I would have persisted in this field if those people had not taken the time to write down their ideas and share it with us all.

From my current perspective, writing in July 2026, it feels like software development has shifted widely from what I experienced a decade ago. At school and during my initial years of development, I had to search through Stackoverflow and tons of other websites to learn anything I needed. It was tough to find the things I needed, but the journey was totally worth it. For example, while trying to figure out a bug around why my Angular component did not work as expected, I would stumble upon extra details about how components should be implemented.

Today one prompt and a click from an LLM immediately provides the solution to the problem. We get to solve the problems quicker, but is something lost in this speed. By not struggling to find the solution to the problem do we lose the knowledge we would have gained by struggling? Do we even pay attention to what is causing the problem if we can copy and paste the issue and get an immediate answer to the problem? I've noticed myself doing that a few times, copying the error from the console and pasting it in an LLM without putting in any effort to fix it.
